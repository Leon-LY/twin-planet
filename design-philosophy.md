# Warm Journal with Twin Foxes

## Space and Form

Two small foxes sit back to back on an open journal page. They share the same den, the same birthday, the same family — yet they are two distinct souls. The negative space between them forms a heart: the invisible bond that every twin family knows intimately. The forms are organic, hand-painted, softened at every edge. Nothing is sharp. Nothing would hurt to touch.

The world they inhabit is a journal — warm paper, dotted lines, washi tape, stamps. Not a cold digital surface, but a page that has been held, written on, loved. The foxes live inside this page. Every UI element is stationery: cards are paper cutouts, buttons are wax seals, dividers are perforated tear lines.

## Color and Material

**Warm sun meets warm earth.** Two gender-neutral earth tones emerge from a field of cream paper:

- **Amber #E07B3E** — the elder twin's color. Turmeric, crushed marigold, the warmth of afternoon sun. (大宝 · 暖阳)
- **Terracotta #C08552** — the younger twin's color. Fired clay, riverbank earth, the calm of warm ground. (小宝 · 暖土)

These are not digital colors; they are pigments ground by hand. The palette whispers rather than shouts. A brighter **Spring green #4FAE6E** marks completion — distinct from the muted terracotta so the eye never confuses a status with an identity. A fleck of **Gold #C8993E** marks achievement. The background carries the faint grain of handmade paper.

**The twin colors follow birth order, never gender.** Amber and terracotta are both earth tones, both neutral. A boy-girl pair, two boys, or two girls — the colors stay the same. The elder is always amber (warm sun energy), the younger is always terracotta (warm earth calm). This is the iron rule.

## The Twin Foxes

Three foxes form the brand's character family:

- **Amber Fox** (大宝守护) — warm smile, round eyes, the elder twin's guardian. Sits slightly forward, curious and bright.
- **Terracotta Fox** (小宝守护) — calm smile, round eyes, the younger twin's guardian. Sits with quiet steadiness, grounded.
- **Gold Fox** (成就使者) — laughing eyes, star sparkles, appears only for achievements and sticker unlocks.

All foxes share the same hand-painted watercolor style: ink outlines with varying thickness, soft watercolor wash, visible paper texture. They are not geometric shapes stacked together — they are painted, with the imperfection and warmth of a real brush.

## Scale and Rhythm

Small things matter most. A fox's face no larger than a fingertip carries an entire emotional state. The rhythm is unhurried: breathe in for three seconds, hold for one, exhale for four. Elements appear not in a grid but as if placed by hand on a journal page — slightly askew, deliberately imperfect, each piece with room to be itself.

## Composition and Balance

Two foxes of equal visual weight sit in symmetry, but the symmetry is broken at the edges — a sticker placed slightly crooked, a stamp rotated two degrees, a tear that wanders off its intended line. These imperfections are the human fingerprint. They prove this was made by hands, not machines. Stamps and washi tape appear only as accents — punctuation marks in the journal, not the main text. The main text is always the foxes and the data they guard.

## Visual Hierarchy

A single handwritten word carries more weight than a paragraph of system text. The greeting speaks first. The twin foxes speak second. Everything else waits its turn. The most important action — recording a moment — occupies the physical and visual center of the page. It is round, warm, inviting. You want to press it.

## Craftsmanship

Every element must appear labored over with care. The fox illustrations are watercolor paintings, not vector geometrics. The sticker cards appear to have been cut by hand with scissors that wandered slightly. The dotted lines were drawn with a pen running low on ink. The faces on the foxes were sketched quickly but with deep affection. Nothing is perfect, and that is precisely what makes it perfect.

## The "Bingdi" Promise

The brand's slogan — "并蒂而生，同步成长" (Born from the same root, growing in sync) — is carried by the foxes' narrative: two foxes born from the same den under a twin lotus, growing together in the same journal. The lotus is not a visual IP of its own; it is the origin story that gives the foxes their meaning. The visual world is the journal. The characters are the foxes. The promise is bingdi.
